1. Scope, company, and privacy roles

This Privacy Policy applies to Browslium LLC ("Browslium," "we," "us," or "our") and the Browslium website, management console, managed applications, device-management functions, protected network and content services, support channels, and related services.

Depending on the supported platform and customer configuration, those services may include an always-on VPN or global proxy and, for Windows, a local agent and service, a production-signed Windows Filtering Platform component, a transparent local HTTPS proxy, and a managed certificate authority. Some platform capabilities remain under development and are available only after required technical, signing, licensing, and platform approvals are complete.

Browslium is offered to individuals, families, businesses, educational institutions, nonprofit organizations, religious institutions, community organizations, and other customers that own or are legally authorized to manage devices. It is not an undisclosed monitoring or payment-enforcement service.

Customers and Browslium

For an individual or family account, the adult account holder determines the authorized filtering purpose and device configuration. For a business, school, nonprofit, religious or community organization, or other entity, that organization generally determines the purpose and permitted configuration. Depending on applicable law, the customer may act as the controller, business, or equivalent decision-maker, and Browslium generally acts as its processor, service provider, or contracted technology provider.

Browslium acts independently for limited business data that it needs to operate its own company and protect the service, such as customer contacts, contract and billing records, website security logs, and support administration. A customer agreement or data-processing addendum may provide additional terms. If those terms provide stronger protections, the stronger protection controls.

2. Data Browslium may receive or process

The exact data depends on the device platform, ownership model, management mode, customer policy, permissions, and features enabled. Browslium limits collection to data reasonably necessary for the contracted service.

2.1 Customer, administrator, and account data

We may process an individual, family, or organization account name; administrator, adult account holder, and authorized contact names; email addresses; account identifiers; roles and permissions; authentication and security events; contract or subscription status; billing contacts; and support history.

2.2 Managed-device inventory

We may receive management and device resource identifiers, enrollment token status, device name, manufacturer, model, serial number, IMEI or other persistent identifiers where device management permits them, operating-system and security-patch versions, ownership and management mode, language, time zone, network state, battery or hardware status, encryption and screen-lock state, and the date and status of enrollment.

Persistent identifiers are used only for legitimate device management, security, enrollment, inventory, support, and compliance. They are not used for advertising or unrelated tracking.

2.3 Applications, policies, and compliance

We may process managed application inventory and installation status, required or blocked application status, policy identifiers and versions, configuration state, security posture, noncompliance reasons, policy-enforcement events, administrative commands, command status, last check-in, tamper or bypass signals, and audit records showing which authorized administrator changed a policy or device state.

2.4 Protected network and filtering events

When Browslium protection is active, we may process domain names, active URLs, IP and connection metadata, request and response headers needed for filtering, protocol and content type, timestamps, application or browser name and process context where technically available, filtering category, rule matched, and the allow, block, replace, or review decision.

This can constitute website content and web-browsing activity. Browslium uses it only to provide, secure, troubleshoot, and report the disclosed content-filtering service. Browslium does not build an unrelated browsing-history profile, use browsing activity for advertising, or track a user across unrelated services.

2.5 Content evaluated for security

Where the customer has lawfully enabled content inspection and the platform technically supports it, Browslium may temporarily process active-page DOM text and metadata, images and thumbnails, video frames or metadata, audio segments or metadata, speech-to-text results, filenames, file types, downloads, PDFs, office documents, embedded content, and classification outputs.

Because digital content may contain sensitive information, sensitive data can be processed incidentally. Browslium does not use that incidental content to profile a person for advertising, sell personal information, or infer characteristics for unrelated purposes.

Browslium is not designed to record keystrokes, saved passwords, password-field values, full payment-card numbers, bank credentials, or authentication secrets. The filtering system is designed to minimize handling of authentication cookies and other credentials and not use them for content classification.

2.6 Diagnostics, security, and support

We may process app version, crash and error reports, connection failures, latency, service health, permission state, installation status, security alerts, fraud or abuse signals, and information voluntarily submitted in a support request, including screenshots and diagnostic files.

2.7 Public website data

This public website does not include advertising trackers or cross-site behavioral advertising. Ordinary hosting systems may process IP address, browser type, requested page, timestamp, and security logs needed to deliver and protect the site. The support form on this site prepares an email in the visitor’s own email application; the form does not transmit the entered text to a separate website database.

2.8 Browser and application network processing

Browslium’s planned Windows core filtering path does not require a browser extension. Supported traffic from compatible browsers and applications can be routed through the Windows network enforcement layer and transparent local HTTPS proxy. The system may process the active request, response, URL, supported page resources, and process context needed to return an allow, block, replace, filter, or review decision before supported content is delivered.

This processing is used only for the disclosed filtering, device-security, support, and compliance functions. Browslium does not use browsing activity for advertising, sale, credit, insurance, employment, or another unrelated eligibility decision.

3. How protected traffic and content are processed

Browslium uses the supported protection path for each authorized device. Android and Apple deployments may use managed VPN, proxy, or platform networking controls. The planned Windows production path uses a local agent and service, a production-signed Windows Filtering Platform enforcement component, a transparent local HTTPS proxy, and a managed certificate authority. It does not require a browser extension for core filtering. When policy enables fail-closed protection, unsupported direct connections or bypass paths may remain unavailable.

  1. The managed device, application, or browser sends supported traffic through the protected path.
  2. Browslium examines the minimum metadata or content needed to apply the customer’s policy. Processing may occur locally and, when a remote classification is required, through Browslium’s protected service.
  3. The remotely processed material may include the active domain or URL, necessary headers, content type, text or document excerpts, an image or video frame, an audio segment, and limited browser or application context. Browslium does not intentionally send passwords, authentication cookies, or password-field values.
  4. The service returns an allow, block, replace, filter, or review decision. Original content payloads are ordinarily discarded after that decision as described in Section 6.
  5. Operational and policy events may be recorded for security, compliance, troubleshooting, and authorized customer reporting.

Browslium does not defeat certificate pinning, digital-rights management, or end-to-end encryption. Customer policy may permit a narrowly scoped direct bypass for an approved critical service, or an unsupported content application may remain unavailable if secure inspection is technically incompatible. Browslium cannot promise inspection of every application, protocol, or encrypted content format.

Private or incognito browser sessions are subject to the same protected network path when the browser and traffic are technically compatible. Browslium does not retain private-session page content as browsing history; any supported content submitted for a filtering decision follows the same transient-content rule described in Section 6.

Limited content use: Browslium uses and transfers content-derived data only to provide or improve the user-facing purpose of content filtering and its security, support, and compliance functions. It is not used for advertising, unrelated profiling, or sale. Human access is limited to cases where the user or authorized customer requests support, access is necessary for security or abuse investigation, law requires it, or data has been aggregated and de-identified.
Important: Browslium is a security filter, not a general-purpose VPN anonymity service. Its purpose is to apply the authorized customer’s disclosed security and acceptable-use rules.

4. Why Browslium uses the data

Browslium uses data only for legitimate service purposes, including:

  • creating and securing customer and administrator accounts;
  • enrolling, inventorying, configuring, and deprovisioning authorized devices;
  • installing approved applications and enforcing customer policies;
  • operating the always-on protected connection and applying content-security decisions;
  • detecting noncompliance, tampering, abuse, outages, and attempts to bypass protection;
  • providing authorized administrators with device, policy, and compliance information;
  • providing onboarding, troubleshooting, privacy, security, and account support;
  • maintaining service reliability, defending legal rights, and meeting legal or contractual duties.

Browslium does not use device management or Windows enforcement to finance devices, collect debts, enforce payment status, secretly monitor users, fingerprint users, eavesdrop, install undisclosed services, or alter devices for resale or lease.

5. When data may be shared

Browslium may disclose only the data reasonably necessary to the following recipients:

  • Authorized account holders and customer administrators, according to their roles and the customer agreement.
  • Platform providers, such as Google Android Enterprise, Android Management API, Android Device Policy, Managed Google Play, Apple device-management services, Microsoft Entra, Microsoft Intune, Windows Autopilot, and other operating-system, signing, distribution, or management services required for enrollment, application distribution, updating, and management.
  • Infrastructure and security providers that host, transmit, store, protect, diagnose, or support the service under contractual and security restrictions.
  • Content-classification providers only when a customer configuration uses them and only to the extent needed for the filtering decision.
  • Professional advisers and authorities when reasonably necessary to comply with law, respond to valid legal process, investigate abuse, protect safety, or defend legal rights.
  • A successor organization in a merger, acquisition, financing, reorganization, or sale, subject to appropriate confidentiality and applicable law.

Browslium does not sell personal information. It does not share managed-device or filtering data for cross-context behavioral advertising.

6. Retention periods

The following are Browslium’s default retention periods unless a customer agreement requires a shorter period or law requires a longer one.

Data categoryDefault retention
Original content payloads used for an automated filtering decisionOrdinarily discarded as soon as the decision is returned. Original images, video, audio, and documents are not placed in the permanent decision cache. Content voluntarily attached to a support or review case is handled with that case and ordinarily deleted within 30 days after closure.
Filtering event logs, URLs, and policy eventsUp to 90 days after creation by default, unless the customer selects a shorter available period or a documented security investigation requires longer retention.
Device inventory, compliance, policy, and enrollment recordsWhile the device is enrolled or the customer account is active, then ordinarily deleted or de-identified within 90 days after authorized deprovisioning or contract termination.
Decision-cache signatures and classificationsA non-reconstructable identifier or signature and its decision may be retained without automatic expiration while needed to avoid reprocessing previously classified content. It is not the original content and is separated from customer-identifying logs after those logs expire.
Security and diagnostic logsUp to 12 months, or longer when reasonably required to investigate an active incident or meet a legal duty.
Support communicationsUp to 24 months after the support case closes, unless a shorter customer term applies.
Contracts, billing, tax, and legal recordsFor the contract term and up to 7 years afterward when needed for accounting, tax, legal, or dispute purposes.
BackupsDeleted data is removed from active systems and ordinarily ages out of encrypted backups within 90 additional days.

Original private-session content is not retained as browsing history. Operating-system, signing, and application-distribution providers are not content-classification recipients and are not intentionally sent the active page content that Browslium evaluates.

Browslium may aggregate or de-identify information so that it no longer reasonably identifies a person or customer. De-identified information may be retained to secure, measure, and improve the service.

7. Deletion, deprovisioning, and privacy rights

Customer-controlled deprovisioning

An adult account holder, device owner, or authorized customer administrator may request that Browslium deprovision a device, remove management, wipe an eligible customer-owned device, delete a device record, or close an account, subject to the ownership model and customer instructions. A wipe may permanently erase the device, and the customer is responsible for confirming authorization and backups before requesting it.

Account and data deletion

A customer or authorized user may start an account or data deletion request through the support request form or by emailing admin@browslium.com. We may verify identity, ownership or management authority, and the scope of the request before acting.

When an eligible account-deletion request is completed, Browslium deletes the associated personal data from active systems rather than merely freezing the account, except for data that must be retained for security, fraud prevention, legal compliance, billing, contractual duties, or dispute resolution. Remaining backup copies age out as described above.

Individual rights

Depending on local law, individuals may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, object to, or obtain a copy of personal information. An individual or family account holder may contact Browslium directly. A user of an organization-managed device should generally contact that organization first because it controls the management purpose and policy. Browslium will assist the customer as required by contract and law.

8. Security and international processing

Browslium uses administrative, technical, and organizational safeguards designed for the sensitivity of the data, including access controls, role separation, authentication, encryption in transit, encryption at rest where appropriate, logging, monitoring, backups, vulnerability management, and restrictions on personnel and service-provider access.

No system can guarantee absolute security. Customers must protect administrator credentials, assign least-privilege roles, promptly remove former administrators, maintain supported devices, and report suspected incidents.

Browslium may process data in countries other than the user’s or customer’s country. Where required, Browslium uses contractual, organizational, or legal transfer mechanisms intended to protect the data. Regional hosting or additional transfer terms may be addressed in the customer agreement when available.

9. Educational customers and minors

Browslium accepts family and educational customers, but children may not independently create or authorize a managed-device account. A parent or legal guardian may authorize Browslium for an eligible family device used by a minor. A school or other authorized organization may deploy Browslium on eligible devices used by minors only when it has the authority, notices, consents, contracts, and policies required by applicable law.

Browslium processes data from those devices only to provide the contracted management, security, filtering, compliance, and support services. Families and educational customers remain responsible for required parental notices, school policies, consents, and other legal requirements.

10. Contact and policy changes

Browslium may update this policy when the product, legal requirements, or data practices change. Material changes will be identified by a new effective date and may also be communicated through the service or customer contacts.
