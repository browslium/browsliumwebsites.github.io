01 / Browslium

A website is more than its address

Allowing a site does not mean every image, video or piece of text on it is right for every person. Browslium is designed to evaluate supported content inside a page, rather than deciding only at the domain level.

08 / FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Clear answers about the design, current limits and support for the project.

What is Browslium?+

Browslium is a content-filtering system in development. It is designed to analyze supported material reaching a user, including images, video, audio, text and documents, rather than relying only on whole-site decisions.

How is Browslium different from a traditional internet filter?+

Domain, URL, category and blocklist controls can be useful. Browslium aims to add analysis of supported items within a page or app according to the selected policy.

Does Browslium only block websites?+

No. Website and URL rules can be part of protection, but the design also evaluates supported individual items so a decision need not apply to an entire website.

What types of content can Browslium filter?+

The architecture covers supported images, video, audio, text, documents, downloads and web content. Exact availability depends on the platform, feature and development stage.

What are filtering levels?+

A filtering level is a set of rules for handling supported content. Different levels let a person or organization choose a policy that fits its needs.

How many image-filtering levels are there?+

The project defines seven image-filtering levels. The interactive section above explains each policy; current availability varies by platform and feature.

How many video-filtering levels are there?+

The project defines six video-filtering levels. Video has separate policies because moving content requires analysis over time.

Why use several levels instead of one setting?+

People and organizations have different standards. Several levels allow a more precise policy than a single on/off switch.

How does image filtering work?+

When a supported image passes through the filtering path, the system is designed to analyze it against the selected image policy and decide how to handle it. The preview above illustrates policies without using inappropriate imagery.

How does video filtering work?+

Browslium is designed to analyze supported video content over time and apply a video-specific policy. Processing moving frames differs from processing a still image; production capabilities remain feature-dependent.

What if the system is uncertain about content?+

Uncertainty is intended to be handled under the chosen safety policy. The response may involve further analysis or more conservative handling, depending on the content type and implementation; no universal production behavior is promised.

Does Browslium also filter audio and text?+

The architecture includes separate analysis and policy logic for supported audio and text. These capabilities are in development and their availability should be checked by platform.

Will one item cause an entire page to be blocked?+

Not necessarily. Where technically supported, the system is designed to distinguish an item from the page containing it so one decision need not determine the outcome for everything else.

Does Browslium work in every app and browser?+

No universal coverage is claimed. It depends on operating-system restrictions, app architecture and the Browslium components available on each platform.

Which platforms are being developed?+

Android, Apple platforms and Windows are in development or testing. See the Platform status section for the current status of each; none is presented here as a general production release.

Will content filtering slow down my internet?+

Analysis takes processing time. The design uses optimized pipelines and caching, but actual delay can vary by content, device, network and the analysis required; no fixed latency is promised.

Is Browslium available today?+

Browslium is in active development and testing. Availability differs by platform and feature; the Platform status section above is the current shared summary.

Is Browslium only for parents?+

No. It is being designed for individuals, families, schools, businesses, nonprofits and community organizations seeking more control over content on their devices.

How much will Browslium cost?+

The project’s goal is to make filtering accessible without charging users for access, supported by voluntary contributions. Final availability and pricing have not been announced.

Why does Browslium ask for contributions?+

Engineering, testing, AI infrastructure, GPU computing, servers, security and operation all have costs. Voluntary support helps fund the ongoing work.

How are contributions processed?+

Contributions use a Stripe-hosted payment page. Browslium’s website does not collect or store payment-card details. A contribution does not purchase a product or service.

Can I contribute every month?+

The current Stripe link accepts a one-time amount chosen by the contributor. A monthly option is not shown because recurring contributions have not been configured.

How does Browslium handle privacy?+

Filtering supported content requires analysis to make a decision. The current Privacy Policy explains this website and voluntary contributions. Product-specific data handling and retention details will be published before a general service release.
